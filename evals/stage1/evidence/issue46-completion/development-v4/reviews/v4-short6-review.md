## Findings by cause

- **d1 — pass.** With West’s SK led, North S7 and East S4, South’s low S3 duck is legal and preserves SA. The initial plan counts the manifest’s five immediate winners correctly (SA, three heart tricks, CA) and proposes a diamond source with heart/club entries. Its hold-up is conditional strategy, not a claim that unknown spade lengths are known.
- **d2 — fail, new root.** At South’s turn the visible trick is SK–S7–S4; South holds SA, S9, S3. SQ, SJ and ST have not been played. The answer says both S9 and SA are “stoppers.” SA is a stopper; S9 is not an established stopper while higher spades remain outstanding. Its future usefulness would require an additional condition (those higher cards removed or positioned), which the plan does not state. This is distinct from d1’s reasonable low-card hold-up.
- **d3–d5 — uncertain, not a proven impossible route.** The repeated text says “using South’s remaining diamond length to regain the lead if needed,” and d4 says “preserving South’s diamond entries.” If this means returning from dummy to South through D5432 opposite North’s KQJT9, it is impossible: South’s diamonds are all lower, and the partnership cannot win a diamond trick in South while North still supplies a higher diamond. But the wording does not name South as the destination; d4’s selected D2 is expressly led “toward dummy’s KQJT9,” a physically valid South-to-North entry, and d6 later describes South’s diamonds as entries “to dummy.” The d3 SA-over-SQ action is correct; d4’s D2 is a plausible lead toward dummy that can lose to the still-unseen DA; d5’s D9 beats West’s D6 while preserving North’s higher honors against East’s possible DA. Because the text admits both a feasible-entry and an impossible-return reading, I would require clarification rather than fail these decisions on the entry phrase alone. This is one repeated ambiguous plan, not three new action errors. The later d3 phrase about leading spades “through South’s remaining S9” is a conditional opportunity, not an assertion that S9 is already a stopper.
- **d6 — fail, new root.** North holds HA43 and South HKQJ: each hand has three hearts. A heart trick requires both hands to follow while both remain nonvoid; after three rounds both declarer hands are out. The four honors therefore cannot make four separate heart tricks. The replacement plan’s “Cash the heart honors for four tricks” is a material winner overcount. Its diamond direction—South’s remaining diamonds as entries to dummy after DA has gone—is physically plausible and is not the defect here.
- **d7–d10 — fail, propagated plan error.** Each `keep` retains the same four-heart-trick overcount after d6. The selected low discards (C5, then HJ, CT, C6) are generally legal. At d8 the trick is a spade trick: West leads SJ, North discards C5, East discards H2, and South discards HJ. “Follow with the lowest heart honor” mislabels HJ as a follow rather than a discard; HJ is a legal discard and may preserve the higher heart honors, but it further reduces the already overcounted heart source. At d9 and d10 the retained four-heart plan remains materially false. These are repetitions of the d6 count root, not new route failures.
- **d11 — fail, new winner-assessment root.** West has led CQ; North is void in clubs and must discard, but South still visibly holds CAJ and plays fourth. CA necessarily beats CQ regardless of East’s card. Calling this “West’s club winner” before South’s play is false. North’s H3 discard itself is legal and can be a reasonable low-heart discard; the failure is the claimed trick outcome and plan built around losing the trick.
- **d12 — fail, realized unnecessary concession.** After CQ–H3–C9, South still has CA and is last to play. CA wins the trick; CJ loses to West’s CQ and leaves CA unused. The replacement plan says to seek the remaining tricks “without giving up further tricks,” so CJ conflicts with its stated aim and sacrifices a sure current winner. Taking CA now permits the visible route through North’s four established diamonds and two further heart winners; preserving it cannot recover the current trick and yields one fewer trick even if every later trick is won. The contract is already in jeopardy at this point, but under the stated IMP objective avoiding this extra undertrick is material. d12 is the consequence of the d11 false-winner premise, but the selected-card concession is its own failing decision.
