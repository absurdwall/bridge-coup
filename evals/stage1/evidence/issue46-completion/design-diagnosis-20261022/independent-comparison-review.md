# Independent review of completed d12 history probes

Scope: read-only review of the completed fixed18, history-study (10 requests), and reasoning-window-study (8 requests) records under /tmp/bridge46-design-diagnosis-20261022. I read the completed result and audit files only; I did not inspect active or future outputs and made no model calls.

## What the completed records support

The clearest observed contrast is between the fresh and full-history conditions at d12. The same current d12 snapshot and the same incorrect carried effectivePlan are present in the fresh normal-play requests; across the two fixed18 full-condition repetitions and the two history-study fresh repetitions, the model chose CA all four times. In the two history-study full-seed repetitions and the two reasoning-window full-seed repetitions, it chose CJ all four times. Every run reports Luna Medium, strict output schema, no tool definitions, and one completed request. Wire audits confirm that after removing per-item IDs and normalizing absent/null reasoning.content, the injected histories match the intended saved histories; public text, ordering, opaque encrypted_content, current input, and fixed request parameters match. That makes a context effect a credible explanation for this sample. It does not establish a general probability or identify which element of the full history matters.

The intermediate-history results do not isolate a stable mechanism. Public-only history chose CA in both history-study repetitions and CJ in both reasoning-window repetitions: pooled, that is two CA and two CJ. The one-item reasoning condition was also mixed, one CA and one CJ. All-but-last chose CJ twice. This does not support a simple claim that the last opaque reasoning item alone drives the error. Nor does it show that public assistant text is harmless: one block produced two CJ choices with public-only history. With two trials per cell, different run timing, and different sampled responses, these are suggestive patterns only.

The records also show that the false CQ-winner conclusion is not exclusive to seeded thread history. In the two fresh d11 precursor requests, one replacement plan says to take the lead “after West wins” CQ, even though South still holds CA and will follow clubs later in the trick; the other plan says South can cover CQ with CA. So prior-thread context is not necessary for that prediction error. The fresh d12 responses choose CA four of four times, however, and do not repeat the bad d12 selection. This distinguishes a d11 plan prediction from a d12 selected-card error and cautions against treating them as one deterministic defect.

The heart probes do not regenerate the original four-heart overcount. Fresh and seeded d6 normal-play outputs avoid that explicit count, while the separate direct fact control answers three maximum heart tricks in both repetitions. The direct club control answers that CA can win in both repetitions. These are two correct probes per fact; the four CA choices above are normal-play d12 trials, not direct club fact questions. The evidence supports available basic knowledge in a direct format, not dependable application in the combined planning task.

## Audit and causal limits

The wire audits support the intended history manipulation: all 10 history requests and all 8 reasoning-window requests have one completed HTTP request, the exact current user snapshot, matching fixed model/reasoning/schema parameters, and an empty tool array. The full, public, last-only, and all-but-last conditions differ in the selected historical items as planned. The normalizations account only for item IDs and an absent versus null optional reasoning.content field. The opaque encrypted reasoning strings were retained and matched by hash when expected. This is strong evidence about request construction, not about what semantic content the model extracted from encrypted reasoning.

Fresh versus full history changes the whole prior context, its length, earlier actions and explanations, and reasoning items together. It therefore cannot attribute the observed split specifically to the wrong d11 plan, to prior public language, or to hidden reasoning. Public-only and reasoning-window differences are too small and inconsistent to separate these. Each request is a fresh sample on a new thread; timing, routing/cache IDs, and sampling can vary even when the audited visible inputs are controlled. The sample sizes are small and sequential, so 4/4 versus 0/4 should be described as a strong bounded contrast, not a reliability rate.

The existing incorrect effectivePlan is not sufficient by itself to force CJ in these observations: the fresh requests have that same carried text and choose CA four of four times. It may still interact with the longer model-visible history. The direct fact controls and fresh choices likewise do not prove a production fix or settle why the original integrated sequence failed.

## Proposed next diagnostic

A one-sentence step-1 intervention is justified as a small diagnostic. It targets the observed contrast without adding card advice or a bridge strategy: instruct the model to treat earlier assistant conclusions as fallible and derive current facts from the latest snapshot before carrying conclusions forward. Test it against a contemporaneous unchanged-prompt control using identical full seeded history and current d12 input. If feasible, retain matched fresh-context controls too. The only changed item should be that sentence in the final active request; leave the prior seeded turns unchanged. Predeclare selectedCard and plan/reason correctness as separate outcomes, and retain all attempts.

The test would tell us whether this explicit re-derivation instruction changes behavior under this specific saved history. It would not prove that history caused the earlier failures or that the sentence is a durable fix. The fresh d11 error shows that current-trick winner reasoning can still fail without prior thread history, while the fixed instructions already say later plays may change winningPlay. Thus a positive d12 result would support a bounded memory-contract/attention hypothesis, not establish that this sentence resolves the separate provisional-winner interpretation. The four-heart error was not reproduced in these normal-play probes, so they do not show that the proposal fixes that failure either.
